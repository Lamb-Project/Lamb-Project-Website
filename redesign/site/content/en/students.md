Your teacher or institution provides the link to your activity, usually inside your learning management system. This project website does not create a student account for you or know which course you belong to.

## How to get in

1. Sign in to your institution's learning platform.
2. Open the course and the activity named by your teacher.
3. Follow the activity's instructions. If access fails, send your teacher or support team the error message, without sharing passwords or session links.

You do not need to create an account with an AI company to use a LAMB activity. You enter through your course.

## What LAMB is designed to give you

LAMB was designed around the [Safe AI in Education Manifesto](/en/safe-ai/), written by the people who build LAMB. These are the parts that concern you most. Some depend on how your institution runs LAMB, and we say so where they do.

**Your teacher stays responsible.** The teacher sets up the assistant and decides what it is for. When an AI helps evaluate your work in a file-evaluation activity, it only proposes a grade. Your teacher sets the final grade, you see nothing until the teacher publishes it, and then you see the AI's proposal and your teacher's grade as two separate lines. A chat assistant may also give you feedback or a score; treat that as practice, not as your grade.

**From LAMB 0.8, you are told when your teacher can read your chats with your name.** If your teacher chose to review chat transcripts, a notice appears the first time you open the activity, before the chat starts. It says that your teachers can read what you write and the assistant's answers, with your name. Entering the chat means you accept this. If you do not agree, do not enter, and talk to your teacher. Even without that option, the teacher who built the assistant can read the conversations held with it in LAMB's analytics, where students appear as numbered users rather than by name. Something you write can still identify you.

**You can tell what the AI wrote.** Every answer in the chat appears under the assistant's name. AI answers can be incomplete or wrong, even when they sound confident. Check important claims against your course materials and ask your teacher when something is unclear.

**The AI company does not get your name.** LAMB does not send your name or email to the model service. What you type in the conversation, and the text of any file you submit for AI evaluation, does reach the model, including any name written in it. If your institution runs the model itself, that text stays with your institution; if it uses an outside provider, the provider receives it under the institution's account. If your course gives you a personal LLM key, the access page explains what is recorded before you take it.

## Who can see what I write?

It depends on how the activity is set up. Ask your institution:

- which model services process your messages;
- whether teachers or administrators can review conversations, and whether with your name;
- how long conversations and submissions are kept, and how to ask for them to be deleted.

Do not assume chats are anonymous. Do not type personal information that the task does not need, about yourself or anyone else.

## When a decision feels wrong

An AI answer or an AI-proposed grade is not your teacher's decision. Follow your course's rules about assessment and AI assistance. The manifesto says students must be able to appeal AI-driven decisions to a person. LAMB has no appeal button of its own: if you disagree with a grade or with feedback, raise it with your teacher through your institution's usual procedure.
